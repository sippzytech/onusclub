# Competitor Landscape — the list to work through

Built 2026-10-18. The companion to [PERKSTAR_ANALYSIS.md](./PERKSTAR_ANALYSIS.md), which
is a deep tear-down of one competitor; this is the map of who else to tear down.

## How to use this

The plan is to visit each site, inventory what it does, and classify every feature
**COPY / DEFER / SKIP** the way the Perkstar doc does. One competitor per session.
Record the outcome in the Status column and write anything substantial into its own
section at the bottom.

⚠️ **A marketing site is not a tear-down.** The Perkstar doc is worth what it is because
it came from 18 screenshots of a real paid sandbox account. Everything learnt from a
homepage is marketing — features that may not exist, numbers with no source, and screens
chosen to flatter. Where a free trial exists, take it; a logged-in hour beats a day of
reading landing pages.

## 🧭 The classification axis: does the customer have to download an app?

**This is our single biggest selling point, so it is the line that sorts this list.**

A company whose customers must install an app is **not competing for the same sale**. A
café choosing between us and them is choosing between two different promises, and ours
("nothing to download — it lands in the wallet they already have") wins that argument on
its own. Perkstar, Tap2 and iStampr all make the same argument, which is how you can tell
it is the category's real dividing line.

So:

- **Direct competitor** — wallet-native, no customer app. Beat them on features.
- **Not a direct competitor** — own consumer app. **Still worth tearing down**, because
  the model difference says nothing about their dashboard, their analytics, their pricing
  structure or their merchant tooling, all of which may be better than ours. Copy freely;
  just do not panic about their user numbers, because those users came through a channel
  we deliberately do not have.

⚠️ One honest caveat: a consumer app is a weakness *for the café's customer* and a
**strength for the network** — an app is how Piggy accumulated 1.3M members who can be
pointed at any of its 1,600 shops. We cannot do that, and should not pretend the trade is
one-sided.

⚠️ **Do not copy claims, only mechanics.** Several of these sites make statistical claims
("5× more reviews", "78% read rate") with no source. We removed our own fabricated
testimonials on 2026-10-18 precisely because that road ends in a misleading-practice
problem. Copy the *feature*; invent none of the evidence.

## Tap2 — first look, 2026-10-18

**The correction first.** `CLAUDE.md` has named Tap2 as a primary competitor since the
start, and yesterday I doubted it was still trading because it did not surface in a
search. **It is trading, and it is the most directly comparable company found so far.**

- **`tap2.ai` / "Tap2Loyal"**, based in **Maastricht, Netherlands** — our market, our
  model, our language.
- **Wallet-native, no customer app.** "Cards live directly in Apple Wallet and Google
  Wallet… added in about 5 seconds, no account creation." Same promise as ours, word for
  word in places.
- **Works with any POS**, plus an "Infrastructure API" for chains — so they are reaching
  upmarket as well as down.

### What they have that we do not

| Feature | Note |
|---|---|
| **Automations** — behavioural triggers | "Post-visit rewards, inactivity reminders, win-back incentives." Ours are *fixed* cron sweeps a merchant cannot compose. → backlog **P9** |
| **Referrals** — "one-scan referral" | **Third competitor with this.** → **P10** |
| **Event cards** — a third card type | For a special event, with reminders and live updates. Genuinely different from stamp/points, and our polymorphic `program_type` + `config_json` was built for exactly this kind of addition. Worth thinking about. |
| **Print materials — branded and *tracked*** | Not just a QR poster (already on our backlog) but **per-poster attribution**: which flyer, table tent or window sticker actually produced sign-ups. That is the same mechanic as Perkstar's UTM item (**P11**) pointed at physical media, and it is a smarter version of the idea than ours. |

### What we have that they do not advertise

Points **expiry** (our FIFO batch engine), a card **design system** with 24 templates,
RFM segmentation with tunable thresholds, per-card **delivery audit with retry**, email
delivery visibility, and Google sign-in on both sides.

### The uncomfortable bit

They also publish an article titled *"Apps vs klantenkaarten: waarom eenvoud altijd wint"*
— apps versus loyalty cards, why simplicity always wins. **That is our positioning,
argued in Dutch, by a Dutch company, already.** "No app to download" is not a
differentiator against Tap2; it is the price of entry. Against them we differentiate on
depth — expiry, design, segmentation, deliverability — and on the Google reviews loop.

⚠️ Separately: **`tap2points.nl` appears to be a different product** (NFC tap-to-earn,
nine sectors, no wallet passes mentioned). Same "Tap2" prefix, different mechanic. Do not
conflate them when doing the full tear-down.

### ⚠️ How tear-downs actually get done — Sanchit signs up, Claude analyses

Asked 2026-10-18 whether Claude could take competitors' free trials directly. **No, and
it is worth writing down why so it is not re-asked each time.**

- **No browser.** Claude can fetch a page's text, not log into an app and click through
  it. A modern dashboard is JavaScript rendered behind auth; fetching it returns an empty
  shell, which is exactly the part of the product worth seeing.
- **Signing up would mean creating an account on a competitor's service under a
  misleading identity**, and most SaaS terms prohibit access by competitors. That is a
  business risk for OnUsClub, not just an etiquette problem.

**The working model is the one that produced PERKSTAR_ANALYSIS.md**: Sanchit takes the
trial and screenshots the dashboard; Claude turns the screenshots into a classified
inventory. Eighteen screenshots produced the most useful competitive document in this
repo, and it took one session.

**What to capture, in this order** (roughly 15 minutes per competitor):

1. The **overview/home** screen — every metric card and chart
2. **Analytics** in full, including any date-range switcher
3. The **customer list**, and one **customer detail** page
4. The **campaign/messaging** composer, especially any automation or trigger builder
5. **Settings** and the **pricing/upgrade** screen — where the paywall sits says what
   they think is valuable
6. Anything labelled referral, tiers, levels, or automations

## Passtastic — public pages, 2026-10-18

⚠️ **Marketing pages only.** The Claude-in-Chrome extension was not connected, so nobody
has been inside the product. Everything here is what they chose to publish.

### Six automations, and we have two of them

| Their automation | Us |
|---|---|
| Welcome push on first scan | ❌ — we create the card and say nothing |
| **Reward-ready push** (fires at the goal) | ❌ — C1's automation twin |
| Win-back at **30 / 60 / 90 days** | 🟡 one sweep, one threshold |
| Birthday / anniversary | ✅ |
| Geofenced reminder | ✅ Day 24 |
| Coupon-to-loyalty bridge | n/a — no coupon type |

🚨 **The useful conclusion: three fixed triggers get most of P9's value for a fifth of the
cost.** P9 is a composable rules engine at 4–5 days. These three are cron jobs at ~1.5
days total and cover what merchants actually ask for. → **C9**. Let demand for *custom*
rules justify P9 instead of assuming it.

### Other gaps worth noting

- **Sign-up form on the café's own website**, on *every* tier. We have `/m/[slug]` and
  nothing embeddable. → **C10**
- **Custom sign-up fields**, gated behind their middle tier — evidence someone pays for
  them. → **C11**
- **70+ card templates** to our 24. The Perkstar doc already concluded diminishing returns
  past ~15, so this is noted, not chased.
- **POS integrations**: Square, Lightspeed, Holded, plus Zapier/Make and API/webhooks on
  the middle tier. ⚠️ Lightspeed matters for NL — it is also what Piggy integrates with.
- **"Online sales of memberships, bundles and store credit at 0.99% + Stripe"** — a
  revenue line we do not have, and a reason memberships pair with billing.

### Their pricing shape, which is more interesting than their prices

Four tiers by **locations**: Single → Multi (3) → Groups (10+) → Enterprise. Then two
add-ons priced separately: **extra location** and **extra scanner**.

We have one price and a boolean premium flag. Theirs grows with the customer and charges
for what actually scales — locations and scanners — rather than for customers or cards.
Worth having in hand whenever pricing is revisited, alongside Joyn's €17/€51/€68 ladder.

⚠️ Two caveats on their numbers: the fetch returned **INR**, almost certainly geo-detected
rather than their European rate (an earlier read showed €39/month), and their own page
disagrees with itself — the calculator and the tier table quote different figures. Do not
quote Passtastic's prices anywhere without re-checking from an EU IP.

The "category pioneer", and **thinner than us on features** — which is the main finding.

customer. **None of them charges per customer or per card** — and neither do we, which is
worth keeping.

## Piggy, Joyn, LoyaltyPass — 2026-10-26

### 🚨 Piggy is not competing for our customer

The assumption was that Piggy, with 1,600 Dutch businesses, was the incumbent squeezing
us. **Their pricing says otherwise: $79 / $299 / $849 per month**, and the Starter tier is
described as *"for brands getting started with loyalty."* Brands, not cafés.

And the product is far broader than a loyalty card — Rewards, **Rules and tiers**, **Gift
cards and Prepaid**, **Vouchers**, **CRM**, **Automations**, **Email marketing**,
**Touchpoints**, **Forms**, and even **employee loyalty** (rewarding staff for hours
worked, reviews, referrals). That is a marketing suite. A single café would find it heavy
and expensive.

**So the Dutch picture is better than feared:**

| | Price | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Piggy** | $79–$849/mo | Broad marketing suite, aimed at brands |
| **Joyn** | €17 / €51 / €68 | App-based network, 3,000 merchants |
| **Tap2** | unknown | Wallet-native, Maastricht — **the real direct competitor** |
| **Us** | €30/mo | Wallet-native, single cafés |

We sit between Joyn's Lite and Connect, and well under Piggy. For a single café that is a
defensible spot. **Tap2 remains the one to worry about**, not Piggy.

### Joyn — confirmed app-based, and one idea worth taking

*"Customers earn points on each purchase in the Joyn app."* No mention of Apple or Google
Wallet anywhere. 2.2M cardholders, 3,000+ merchants, 1.2M transactions/month.

💡 Their **middle tier's differentiator is "automated VAT export for bookkeeping"** — not
a loyalty feature at all, but admin relief, and apparently what makes a merchant upgrade.
A reminder that for an SMB owner the adjacent chore can sell better than the core product.
→ **C18**, with a hard rule attached: our amounts are optional and loyalty-only, so it can
never be labelled a tax document.

### ⚠️ LoyaltyPass — reclassified, I had this wrong

Listed in Tier 1 on the strength of a search result saying *"$99/month, 14-day free trial,
no credit card required."* **Their own site says solutions start at $9,500 as a one-time
build**, scoped after discovery, with an ongoing partnership and no trial described.

That is an **agency / custom-build model**, not SMB self-serve. Different buyer, different
price by two orders of magnitude. Moved out of Tier 1.

The lesson for the rest of this list: **the $99 figure came from LoyaltyPass's own
comparison blog**, which also ranks LoyaltyPass first in every article. Competitor
comparison content is marketing. Price a competitor from its own pricing page or not
at all.

💡 **One good idea from them anyway:** *"timing-based win-back for cycle-driven
businesses."* Gone-quiet is not a fixed number of days — a café's quiet is two weeks, a
dentist's nine months. We apply 30/90/5 to everyone. → **C16**, and `classifyRfm()`
already takes thresholds as a parameter, so the thinking is done and only the form is
missing.

They also claim **~90% open rates for wallet push against ~20% for email**. Unsourced, so
do not repeat it — but the direction supports the wallet-first choice, and it is the
argument *for* C17 keeping push as the primary channel with email as the fallback for
customers who deleted the pass.

## Final wave — Boomerangme, Stamp Me, 7Stamp, Stampioo, Wallet Loyalty, FaveCard (2026-10-26)

**The sweep is complete.** Eleven companies looked at; the long tail of Tier 1 is not
worth individually visiting — the last six produced six ideas between them and heavy
repetition, which is itself the finding.

### 🚨 Correction: one of them DOES charge per customer

I wrote earlier that "none of them charges per customer or per card, and neither do we."
**Wallet Loyalty does**: €19 per location per month including 500 customers, then **€5 per
additional 500**. Growth-priced on the thing that grows. Noted because it was stated as a
category fact and it is not one.

### The best single idea found: AI onboarding

**Boomerangme's "Richie"** takes a **Google Business Profile link** and builds the card,
the rules and the first messages from it. Paste a link, get a working programme.

Ours is a four-step checklist filled in by hand. The profile supplies name, category,
address and photos — enough to pick a template, suggest a reward, set a stamp count and
draft a welcome. **We already have the AI integration, the template gallery and Google
link parsing.** → **C19**, and it is the feature that would justify turning
`ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` on in production.

### Wallet Loyalty — the closest competitor by shape

Copenhagen, **€19 per location**, site and counter posters in **12 languages including
Dutch**. Stamps, points, prepaid and membership cards.

🚨 Their automation list is **exactly C9**: *welcome, reward ready, birthday, win-back,
nearby*. Second independent confirmation of that precise set — C9 is not a guess.

💡 **"Scheduled messages are sent in each customer's language."** We draft per *merchant*
(NL or EN from `merchants.country`), so an Amsterdam café sends its English, Dutch and
Spanish regulars the same language. → **C21**, nearly free given the Day 28 drafter
already takes a language parameter.

### Friction: we ask for more than anyone else

- **7Stamp:** "registration isn't required", join in ~15 seconds
- **Wallet Loyalty:** "customers never need to provide an email, phone number or name"
- **Us:** name **plus** phone or email, required

At a counter with a queue, every field costs sign-ups. The counter-argument is real — no
contact details means no win-back, no birthday, no email — so → **C22** as a *merchant
choice*, not a new default.

### 🔴 The sweep's single most valuable output was a bug in our own product

PassKit sells **"device-aware landing pages"** — detect the device, serve the right pass.
Checking whether we do that found that **`/m/[slug]` offers only "Add to Google Wallet"**
on its success screen. Apple Wallet has worked since Day 11–12, and `/c/[qrToken]` offers

### Square Loyalty — closer to us than expected

**Apple Wallet passes are supported** (no Google Wallet mentioned). Customers identify with
a **phone number at the till**. **Tiered VIP Bronze/Silver/Gold at 1.5× / 2× / 3×** — that
is C5 and C3 in one. Email and text campaigns via Square Marketing. 30-day trial.

🚨 **One dashboard metric we structurally cannot copy:** *"average spend of loyalty versus
non-loyalty customers."* Square is the till, so it sees every transaction. We only see
loyalty scans, and only when staff type an amount. Worth knowing: a POS-attached
competitor can always out-measure us on program ROI, which is an argument for C2
(campaign attribution, which we *can* do) over chasing program-level ROI we cannot.

**Toast** and **Lightspeed** both returned 403 to automated fetching. Not retried.

### Lightspeed + Piggy, and a correction

Lightspeed officially lists **Piggy** for Restaurant K-Series (points, vouchers, tiers,
gift cards at the POS, no coding). And ⚠️ **Piggy is bigger than recorded**: press and job
listings say **10,000+ businesses including Vapiano, SPAR and Yoghurt Barn**, not the
1,600 first found. Based in Maarssen.

That reinforces rather than changes the conclusion — Vapiano and SPAR are chains. Piggy is
after brands; we are after the single café.

### 💡 Competitive ammunition: what Dutch consumers say about Piggy

**SuperCards** turned out not to be a competitor at all — it is a free consumer app that
stores other people's loyalty cards, with a Dutch "Spaargidsen" SEO content operation.
Their Piggy guide is unusually useful, because it is written for *consumers*:

- Login errors and **disappearing points** through 2024–2025; they advise keeping a
  physical card as backup
- Returns of only ~1–3%
- Points are **store-locked** — not combinable, transferable or cashable
- 🚨 **"If a shop leaves Piggy, its points may become worthless"**
- No retroactive points for missed scans; no rounding (€5.95 earns 5 points)

That last-but-one point is the sharpest argument for our model that exists: **on a network,
the customer's balance depends on the network; on our cards it belongs to the café.** Not a
feature to copy — copy for the *marketing site*.

### PassKit — infrastructure, and a checklist of pass capabilities

B2B pass infrastructure (APIs, SDKs, webhooks), not an end-user product. Useful as a
catalogue of what a wallet pass can do:

- **Device-aware landing pages** → found B1
- **Webhooks on pass events** (install, scan, redemption) → confirms **C12**
- Email distribution of passes, unique pass URLs, CSV batch issuance
- A **usage-based pricing calculator** on the marketing site

**Passcreator, Passworks, Airship, Vibes** — same category, not individually visited. The
category's ideas are represented by PassKit.

### Two conclusions worth keeping

**① C25 is confirmed three times over.** WalletThat's "universal smart link", PassKit's
"device-aware landing pages" and Airship's "adaptive links" are the same idea from three
independent vendors, two of them enterprise infrastructure companies. **This is how the
industry solves the problem B1 exposed** — not two buttons, one link that resolves per
device. Build both anyway: detection fails on desktop and in in-app browsers.

**② The Zettle finding argues for C12 over C14.** The actual way a small loyalty tool
reaches a POS is a **third-party workflow connector**, not a native integration — Loopy
reaches Zettle through `workload.co`, not through code Loopy wrote. That is the whole case
for **outbound webhooks (C12)** instead of building Lightspeed/Square integrations (C14).
One week of work answers "do you integrate with X" for every X, permanently.

**Nothing further to research.** The next step is choosing from the shortlist.
